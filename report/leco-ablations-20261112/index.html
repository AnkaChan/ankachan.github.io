Findings

  1. History window (new, 2026-11-21): the network needs one iteration of history; more gives a small gain. With no history input (window 0) every rollout breaks within 6 to 453 time steps. Windows 2, 3 and 5 lower the median end gradient norm to 0.88×, 0.77× and 0.75× of the baseline (window 1) and converge faster in the first 5 to 10 iterations of the fixed-state test. The gain is of the same size as the gain from width 256 or from 3 message-passing rounds, at +0.5 % to +2 % parameters. One training per model: the order between 2, 3 and 5 is not resolved. See History window.
  2. History window × latent width (new, 2026-11-21): a wider network adds little on top of a longer window. The median end gradient norm with windows 3 and 5 is 0.77× and 0.75× at width 96, 0.77× and 0.79× at width 128, and 0.71× and 0.73× at width 192 (window 1: 1.00×, 0.92×, 0.83×). The gains do not multiply, and width 192 costs 3.9× the parameters. See window × width.
  3. Hinge edges and more inputs (contact gradient, inertia gradient, current position, material through FiLM): none of them beats the baseline. All are level at 15 to 30 iterations; at 5 iterations only the inertia input stays level and the others are worse. The material FiLM model is the one that never tangled on 00756 in three runs.
  4. Initial guess of a time step: starting the iterations with gravity added (x + dt v + dt² g) or with the adaptive warm start of vertex block descent is not better than the baseline start x + dt v. Both are level at 15 to 30 iterations, slightly worse at 5 iterations, and tangle more often on 00756.
  5. New ablations of the block and of the output direction: only one is better than the baseline per iteration: the standard GNN block with edge latents. It is lower in ratio on most sequences and never tangled on 00756 in three runs, but it costs 3.3 times the time per iteration, and at equal time the baseline is ahead. Without neighbour information (context window 0) the model is worse on every sequence, most at few iterations. Attention over the 1-ring is level with the neighbour mean and 38 % slower per iteration. Dividing the output direction by its RMS is level; a unit direction per vertex is much worse; neither rescues the per-node scale.
  6. Five-sequence benchmark (00756, 00396, cc_05_06, cc_05_08, cc_05_16) with the example configurations: the baseline is level with or better than every other width, and the per-node scale models fail on all five. Context windows 3 and 5 are lower in ratio on two sequences. Per-node gradient normalization is level or worse. On 00756 a run either stays clean or tangles: in three runs each the released checkpoint tangled 3 times and the baseline once, while context window 3 and per-node normalization with the magnitude input never did.
  7. A per-vertex step scale does not work at inference. Every model with a per-node scale breaks within the first 60 to 180 of 4,040 steps. This holds at 5, 10 and 30 iterations, with DAT and without DAT, with per-node gradient normalization, with the gradient magnitude input, and with a ring context of 1 to 32 rings. The truncated scale does not rescue them: the positions stay finite, but more than 5 % of the edges are badly stretched after 60 to 180 steps.
  8. Per-node gradient normalization with a global scale works. It finishes every setting. At 5 iterations it is at least as good as the baseline (end distance 21 to 26 mm against 32 to 49 mm, depth 1.6 to 2.9 mm against 1.8 to 10.3 mm). At 10 and 30 iterations the two are equal. Two runs per model are not enough to call it better, and on the criteria of the paper (gradient norm and residual ratio per step) it is level with the baseline.
  9. The gradient magnitude input does not help. With global normalization it makes the 5-iteration result clearly worse (109 to 116 mm from the reference at 25 % of the sequence). At 10 iterations it equals the baseline. With per-node normalization the result is about the same as the baseline.
  10. Width 96 or more is safe. Below 96 the 5-iteration solver is not reliable. Widths 96, 128, 192 and 256 give the same quality. Width 64 keeps good edge numbers but loses a garment at 5 iterations. Width 32 breaks, width 24 gives NaN, width 16 ends far off. At 10 iterations every width finishes. No width above 96 is better than 96.
  11. The other ablations confirm the main design. Each removed input makes the result worse or unstable. The per-iteration scale and the model without scale finish, but with lower accuracy. A context window of 3 equals the baseline within the run-to-run spread.
  12. A context window of 5 gives the closest 5-iteration result. Its end distance is 20 to 21 mm, against 32 to 49 mm for the baseline and 21 to 26 mm for per-node normalization. It costs 5 message-passing rounds per iteration, so it is not a free gain.
  13. The fixed-state test of the code base separates two things. Almost every model can solve a single time step in 30 iterations, also the per-node scale models. Their failure appears only in rollouts. From a cold start the per-node normalization with the magnitude input is the fastest model (6 % of the energy decrease missing after 5 iterations, baseline 31 %).
  14. On the criteria of the paper no ablation beats the baseline at 5 iterations. Objective, gradient norm and residual ratio per time step are level for all stable models. At 10 iterations the larger context windows are about 30 % lower; at 30 iterations all are equal.

Still open: the model without iteration (in training).

broken

History window

How many earlier iterations the network reads. Window K gives the network the gradient and the applied update of each of the last K iterations, next to the current gradient. The baseline is window 1 (last gradient, last update). Window 0 reads the current gradient only; the accumulated step scale stays, because it is a state of the optimizer and not an input. Every old gradient is divided by the RMS of the current gradient, every old update by the step scale of the last iteration, so the network sees how the sizes changed. Only the first layer grows (576 weights per extra pair). Everything else equals the baseline: width 96, one message-passing round, same data, 500,000 training steps, trained without DAT. Benchmark columns: geometric mean over 00396, cc_05_06, cc_05_08 and cc_05_16 of the per-sequence median relative to the baseline (below 1 is better). 00756 can tangle, so it has its own column: runs that stayed clean, of three. Fixed-state columns: share of the energy decrease that is still missing after 5, 10 and 30 iterations (lower is better), and the gradient norm after 10 iterations relative to the start. Green and red mark clear differences against the baseline.

Reading: window × width

  • A wider network adds little on top of a longer window. At width 128 the gain of the window is the same as at width 96 (0.77× and 0.79× against 0.77× and 0.75×). At width 192 it is a little larger (0.71× and 0.73×). If the two gains multiplied, width 192 with window 3 would reach about 0.64×.
  • The step from width 96 to width 192 with a window of 3 or 5 gives 3 to 8 % in the median gradient norm for 3.9× the parameters. Each model has one training, so this is inside the spread between trainings.
  • The residual ratio does not move: 0.89× to 1.01× for all window and width pairs.
  • The fixed-state test follows the window and not the width: after 5 iterations 22 to 23 % is missing at window 3 and 14 to 18 % at window 5, at every width (window 1: 31 to 32 %).
  • One place where both together help: the 5-iteration run on 00396. The median gradient norm is 0.96 for the baseline, 0.84 and 0.86 for windows 3 and 5 at width 96, and 0.65 and 0.71 at width 192. Width alone is worse there (1.14 at width 128, 1.15 at width 192). This is one sequence and one training per model.
  • 00756: windows 3 and 5 at width 192 stayed clean in 3 of 3 runs; width 128 gave 2 of 3 (window 3) and 0 of 3 (window 5). The pinch is random, so this does not rank the models.

Reading

  • Window 0 (no history input) does not work. It learns worse (training loss 2.81 against 0.23) and every benchmark run stops after 6 to 453 time steps. From a fixed state it still lowers the energy, but slowly: 4.2 % of the decrease is missing after 30 iterations, where the baseline misses 0.0 %.
  • The table “Network inputs” has the two cases between window 0 and window 1. Without the previous gradient the model finishes with a 1.3× higher gradient norm. Without the last update it finishes with a 3.5× higher gradient norm. Without both it fails. The network needs one iteration of history, and the last update is the more important part.
  • Windows 2, 3 and 5 give a small gain over window 1. All three finish every run on the four stable sequences. The median gradient norm at the end of a time step is 0.88×, 0.77× and 0.75× of the baseline. The residual ratio stays level (1.01×, 0.92×, 0.94×). The mean over the time steps shows no gain (1.18×, 0.95×, 0.89×), because a few hard time steps set the mean.
  • The three criteria of the paper, per sequence (windows 2, 3 and 5 against window 1):
    • Objective G: level on the four stable sequences (within 1.5 % in the mean and 1.2 % in the median). On the clean runs of 00756 the mean is 8 to 12 % lower with a longer window; the median is within 2 %.
    • Gradient norm, median: windows 3 and 5 are lower on 00396, cc_05_08 and cc_05_16 (0.56× to 0.79×); window 2 is between 0.72× and 1.03× there. All are level on cc_05_06 (1.02× to 1.06×) and near level on the clean runs of 00756 (0.90× to 1.08×).

Limits of these results

  • One test scene. One or two runs per cell. Two runs of the same model differ visibly (see the baseline rows), so small differences between models mean nothing.
  • No timing on this page. The inference runs shared the GPUs with the trainings, so their step times are not clean. Models of other widths also cannot use the fused width-96 network path yet.
  • No model was trained with DAT. DAT, the 7 mm radius and the truncated scale act at inference only.
  • The baseline row is width_96, retrained with the same configuration as all ablations (neighbor_projection: true, node_update_input: combined).
  • Related report: the same network on a tetrahedral cuboid, without retraining.
