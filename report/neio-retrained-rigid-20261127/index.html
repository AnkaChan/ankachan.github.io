NeIO on Newton's examples: the retrained rigid network

Neural Intrinsic Optimizer in place of SolverVBD inside Newton's own examples. Rerun of 2026-11-26 and 2026-11-27 (Pacific time).

Summary

  • What this is. A rerun of the Newton examples that have rigid bodies, with the retrained rigid network of NeIO. 24 of the 33 examples of the earlier page have bodies. The other nine have only cloth or soft bodies; the rigid network does not act on them, so they are not on this page.
  • Retrained network (rigid_two_scales in the port): training run abl_two_scales, checkpoint at step 40,000 of 500,000. The training has contact stiffness from 300 to 3e8 N/m, joint stiffness from 30 to 6e7 N/m, and the joint and contact dampers of SolverVBD in half of the worlds. At step 40,000 the ramp of the contact stiffness is at about 40 %. The network has two step scales (translation, rotation) and reads a rotation as an arc length at the radius of gyration of the body.
  • Earlier network: rigid_e at step 270,000, the network of the earlier page.
  • Two starts of the iterate. The default of the port is the inertial target with gravity (inertia-full). The networks trained with the start x + dt v (inertia-velocity-only).
  • Result. The retrained network with the start x + dt v has 4 blow-ups and 29 runs at or below 5 %. rigid_e with the default start has 5 and 26. basic_shapes, the example with a contact stiffness of 1e8 N/m and a contact damping of 5e5 N s/m, goes from 21.49 % to 2.89 % with 5 iterations, and from 251 % to 0.12 % with 30 iterations.
  • The retrained network needs the start x + dt v. With the default start, the five-box stack of the demo scenes flies apart and some cable examples fail. The cause is under Why the start matters.
  • Not on this page. The training finished on 2026-11-27 at 11:09 Pacific time. The checkpoint at step 500,000 exists; it is not run here.

How to read the page

  • The scene of an example is not changed. Each example runs as it ships; where it constructs SolverVBD, it gets a SolverNeIO. All other settings are those of the earlier page: joints as a plain penalty, one normalisation group per world, history carried over the time steps, the cubic contact law with a band of 5 mm for body-body contact.
  • Two iteration counts per example: the count of the example itself (1 to 30), and 30.
  • Mean body distance to the shipped run: the distance of each body to its position in the shipped run, mean over all frames and all bodies, in percent of the scene size (the diagonal of the bounding box at the first frame).
  • The distance is not an error. The shipped run is not a converged reference, and it uses another contact law. An earlier check against SolverVBD with 100 iterations showed that the shipped runs of cable_y_junction and vbd_dat_rigid_soft are not converged.
  • Three classes. Blow-up: the state became not finite and the run stopped. Above 5 %: another motion, or a failure that stays finite. At or below 5 %: close to the shipped run. balance_bird has one body and no scene size, so it has no number.
  • Error left: for every time step, the gradient norm at the final iterate divided by the gradient norm at the first iteration; the median over the steps. 100 % means no progress.
  • Videos. Each video has four panels that play in step. Top left: the shipped run. Top right: rigid_e with the default start. Bottom left: the retrained network with the default start. Bottom right: the retrained network with the start x + dt v. The configuration rigid_e with the start x + dt v has numbers only.

Why the start matters

  • With the default start (the inertial target with gravity), a body in free flight has no gradient.
  • The retrained network then lowers its translation step scale to 1e-18 within twelve time steps. The rotation step scale stays near 1e-8.
  • At the first contact, the rotation scale rises to 0.8 m within one time step. The translation scale does not recover. The boxes of the stack then turn by about 1 rad per iteration.
  • With the start x + dt v, each time step has the gravity step to make. This keeps the step scale alive, and the stack stands.
  • Cross-check: the solver of the training code with this checkpoint also keeps the stack at rest. The checkpoint is sound.

One difference between the training and the port

  • Training: the contact damper acts on the growth of the penetration over the time step. Its force is zero at the moment a contact closes.
  • Port: the contact damper is that of SolverVBD: the damping constant times the approach speed of the contact points, in full as soon as the contact carries load.
  • The port is not changed. This is an open decision.
