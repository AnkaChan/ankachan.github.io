ALM-DAT on the collision-fidelity benchmark: headline videos

Two recordings of the six headline cases on the Horde L40 VM. Recording 2 (2026-11-06) is the current code, ankac/alm-dat-main at cfd6e07b5, VBD against ALM-DAT. Recording 1 (2026-11-05) is the first ALM-DAT revision with its ablation columns. Page built 2026-11-06.

Each video shows the same case under several configurations, side by side, 1280x720 per panel, 30 frames per second, real time. A panel whose run stopped early holds its last frame behind a red bar that names the stop time and the reason (nonfinite = a non-finite state was observed). Hold outcomes are the benchmark's own criteria: "lost at t" is the first proven hand-object separation. Jitter is the worst detrended RMS displacement over the complete 0.5 s stationary windows, speed the worst relative RMS speed over those windows. Rigid-soft DAT is off in every ALM-DAT configuration because the ALM-DAT path does not yet run together with it; the "VBD, rigid-soft DAT off" column of recording 1 is the control for that difference.

Recording 2 (2026-11-06): current code, VBD against ALM-DAT

Branch ankac/alm-dat-main at cfd6e07b5: today's main, the #4222 self-contact storage and the ALM-DAT self-contact with the decisions of the design review: D1 (the committed displacement from the detection pose stays inside the sphere of radius 0.5 x 0.85 x (margin + gap)), D3 (two detections per substep, the second at the certified anchor), D16 (the row pushes along the stored division-plane normal) and D15 (the row metric is the pair's support along that normal, inertia in series with the material stiffness; the stiffness floor and the user scale are gone). Default detection gap. Six cases, two columns, twelve runs, all twelve finite.

What changed against recording 1.
  • Garments hold at the default reach. Both towels and the jacket are held to the release under ALM-DAT. Recording 1 lost them at the lift (5.50 s, 6.47 s, 7.53 s); the 3 mm gap variant is no longer needed for that and was not rerun.
  • Bags hold without any floor variant. ALM-DAT holds the tote and the gusset bag to the release; VBD loses them at 20.07 s and 31.10 s. Recording 1 needed the no-floor ablation for this; the floor no longer exists. After the release the gusset bag stays caught on one open finger to the end of the run; the tote drops.
  • The cloth stack does not rest. New, and the one regression: the ten-layer stack ripples at 1 to 2 m/s relative speed to the end of the run, where recording 1 rested at 3 to 6 mm of jitter. One run; the responsible change (D3, D15 or D16) is not established.
  • Self-crossings. Fewer confirmed crossing pairs under ALM-DAT on the H1 towel, both bags and the jacket; more on the Franka towel (5 pairs in 118 frames against 1 pair in 12 frames).
  • Wall time. Lower under ALM-DAT in every case, but rigid-soft DAT is off in the ALM-DAT runs and on in the VBD runs, so this is not a solver comparison (the control column of recording 1 shows that effect alone).

Recording 1 (2026-11-05): first ALM-DAT revision and its ablations

Branch ankac/alm-dat-fable-review at 986b7c921 with the uncommitted ALM-DAT changes of that day (friction, damping, smooth compliant-to-hard transition, end-point motion budget). Every ALM-DAT panel uses the distance budget rule with one self-contact detection per substep, and the metric floor rho >= K of that revision.

What these videos show

  • Speed cap. One self-contact detection per substep bounds the committed motion to half the detection reach per substep. For the cloth recipes (3 mm reach at 300 Hz) that is 0.38 m/s. The Franka lift peaks at 0.49 m/s, so every garment slips at the lift. A 3 mm detection gap (5 mm reach) raises the cap to 0.64 m/s and the garments hold, at a steep price in records (table below). The design review chose a second detection at the certified anchor instead (decision D3), which doubles the cap at the same reach; recording 2 runs with it.
  • Metric floor. The ALM row's penalty metric is floored at the contact stiffness. On light payload tetrahedra this makes the multiplier update overshoot and the run goes non-finite within seconds. With the floor off, ALM-DAT completes the bag cases and holds the bags. The floor was replaced by the pair-support metric (decision D15); recording 2 runs with it.
  • Soft stacks. ALM-DAT rests quieter than the penalty path with zero confirmed self-crossings in this recording. Recording 2 does not repeat this: without the floor the stack does not rest.
  • Jacket. The native jacket mesh self-crosses under every solver; that is the mesh, not the contact.
  • Budget rule. The first ALM-DAT revision used a path-length style bound (|offset| + |step| ≤ B) that froze vertices at the cap and sank the cloth. It was removed on 2026-11-05 (decision D1); the end-point rule shown here is now the only behaviour.
